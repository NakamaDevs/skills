# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 profile

Use this reference for strict reviews, rule citations, or questions about differences between the practical profile and the standard.

- Standard: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9
- Issue date: 2025-01-15
- Official PDF: https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf

## Sentence limits

- Procedures, warnings, cautions, and other safety instructions: 20 words, under Rule 5.1.
- Descriptive text and procedure notes: 25 words, under Rules 6.3 and 5.1 guidance.
- Practical communication profile: target 20 words for all prose when accuracy and readability permit.

The practical profile is intentionally more restrictive for descriptive sentences. Do not describe its universal 20-word limit as an Issue 9 rule.

## Voice and verb exceptions

Rule 3.6 requires active voice. Descriptive writing can use passive voice when the agent is unknown.

Do not invent an agent. An invented agent can make the technical statement incorrect.

Issue 9 permits these verb forms:

- Infinitive
- Imperative
- Simple present
- Simple past
- Simple future
- Past participle as an adjective

Avoid complex auxiliary constructions and most `-ing` forms. Preserve an `-ing` form when it is an approved technical noun or modifier.

## Controlled words and technical terms

Formal compliance requires more than short sentences. Check each general word against the Issue 9 dictionary for:

- Approval status
- Part of speech
- Approved meaning
- Approved verb or adjective form

Use official company, industry, or subject-field terms as technical nouns and technical verbs when the rules permit them.

Do not replace a term only because it is absent from the controlled dictionary. First determine whether it is an approved technical term.

## Strict review process

1. Classify the text as procedural, descriptive, safety, quoted, or machine-readable content.
2. Preserve all required technical facts and official terminology.
3. Check sentence structure, verb form, voice, terminology, and word count.
4. Check general words in the Issue 9 dictionary.
5. Check technical terms against the applicable company or domain glossary.
6. Report each issue with its rule number and a proposed correction.
7. Separate confirmed violations from recommendations or unresolved terminology questions.

Use one of these verdicts:

- **Compliant:** The checked text follows the applicable rules and dictionaries.
- **Conditional:** Structure follows the rules, but one or more technical terms need glossary confirmation.
- **Gap:** The text has confirmed rule or dictionary violations.
- **Not assessed:** The request did not include the sources required for a strict compliance claim.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim certification. The skill provides writing assistance and review.
- Do not treat all ordinary communication as aerospace technical documentation.
- Do not change legal, safety, or contractual meaning to satisfy a style rule.
- Do not copy large parts of the standard into the response. Cite the rule and summarize it.
